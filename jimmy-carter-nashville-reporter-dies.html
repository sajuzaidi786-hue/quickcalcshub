Jimmy Carter, Nashville Country Music Reporter, Dies at 73

Jimmy Carter, the longtime Nashville entertainment reporter, died on October 13 at 73. Here is who he was, what he covered and how Nashville remembered him.

Updated October 15, 2026. This is a short factual summary based on published reports. Details may change as more information is confirmed.

Which Jimmy Carter is this?

Searches for his name often bring up the 39th US President of the same name, who died in December 2024. They are two different people. This article is about the Nashville broadcaster.

What happened

Several outlets reported that Carter died on Tuesday, October 13, at age 73. His granddaughter told reporters that he passed away in his sleep, and his family confirmed the news to WSMV. WSM Radio, where he was a regular guest, posted a tribute calling him a cherished member of the WSM family.

His career

Carter started in radio as a teenager and went on to spend more than five decades in broadcasting, production and journalism. He joined WSMV-TV in Nashville in 1982 and, across two stints, spent 21 years as the station's entertainment reporter, covering award shows, movie premieres and Music Row events.

His interviews included country stars such as Dolly Parton, Alan Jackson and Garth Brooks, and film figures such as Clint Eastwood, Sylvester Stallone and Kevin Costner. His credits also included NBC, ABC, CBS, TNN and BBC Scotland, and contributions to the syndicated country program "Crook & Chase". In recent years he was a frequent voice on WSM's "Coffee, Country & Cody" and produced a daily live call-in feature for other country morning shows.

Why he mattered to country music fans

Music reporters are often the link between artists and listeners. Carter was known for deep industry relationships, humor and storytelling, which made him a trusted figure for artists, colleagues and audiences. WSM said he was a trusted source and a gifted broadcaster.

Funeral and memorial details

At the time of writing, we have not found confirmed funeral or memorial arrangements. Check WSM Radio, WSMV or the family's announcements for official updates.
