How Much Concrete Do I Need for a Patio? (Real Example)

A step-by-step way to measure, calculate and order concrete for a patio, with a full 12 x 14 ft example and the mistakes that cost people money.

Order too little and you get a cold joint halfway through the pour. Order too much and you pay for concrete you dump. Here is how to get the number right, using a real example.

Step 1: Measure the area, not the plan

Measure length and width in feet. For a patio that is not a rectangle, split it into rectangles and add them. Then check the depth of the dug-out area in several spots. Uneven ground is the biggest reason people run short.

Step 2: Choose the thickness

A standard patio slab is 4 inches thick. If a heavy grill, hot tub or occasional vehicle will sit on it, ask a local pro about 5 to 6 inches.

Step 3: Do the math

Cubic feet = length (ft) x width (ft) x thickness (in) / 12. Divide by 27 for cubic yards.

Example: a 12 x 14 ft patio, 4 in thick. 12 x 14 x 4 / 12 = 56 cubic feet = 2.07 cubic yards. Add 10% for waste and uneven ground: 61.6 cubic feet = 2.28 yards.

Mistakes that cause problems

  • No slope: a patio needs a slight fall away from the house, commonly about 1/8 to 1/4 inch per foot, so water does not pool.
  • No joints: concrete cracks as it shrinks. Control joints cut roughly every 8 to 10 feet in a 4 in slab help cracks form in straight lines.
  • Soft base: compact 4 inches of gravel under the slab. A soft base leads to settling.
  • Adding water to make it easier: extra water weakens the finished concrete.

Ordering tip

Have forms, base and helpers ready before the truck arrives. The concrete starts setting as soon as it is mixed, and you do not want to be still leveling the ground when it arrives.
