How Long Does Concrete Take to Dry and Cure?

Concrete is walkable in a day or two but takes about 28 days to reach design strength. See the timeline and how weather changes it.

People say concrete "dries", but it actually hardens through a chemical reaction with water, called curing. That is why keeping it damp early helps it get stronger.

Typical timeline

TimeWhat you can usually do
24 to 48 hoursLight foot traffic once the surface is hard
About 7 daysLight vehicles, in many cases. Wait longer for heavy loads
About 28 daysConcrete reaches its design strength. Heavy loads and sealers

These are typical figures for standard mixes in mild weather. Your mix, thickness and weather can change them.

Drying vs curing

The surface may look dry in a day, but the inside is still gaining strength for weeks. Strength keeps rising slowly after 28 days.

Weather matters

  • Cold: below about 40°F (5°C), concrete gains strength slowly and can be damaged if it freezes early. Use blankets or cold-weather mixes.
  • Hot and windy: water evaporates too quickly, which causes cracks. Pour early in the day and keep the surface damp.
  • Rain: cover fresh concrete. Rain on an unfinished surface can leave it weak and dusty.

How to cure concrete well

  1. Finish the surface, then protect it from sun and wind.
  2. For the first 7 days, keep it moist by light spraying, wet burlap, plastic sheeting or a curing compound.
  3. Do not add water to the surface while finishing.
  4. Avoid de-icing salts during the first winter.

When can you seal it?

Many sealer makers recommend waiting about 28 days, but follow the product label.
